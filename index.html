AI and CyberSecurity:

Role in contributing to cyber defence and cyber crime — Friend or Foe?

How AI facilitates cyber crime?

AI automates the time‑consuming tasks cyber criminals require to conduct an attack. Specifically, the reconnaissance stage of information gathering. Previously, researching attack surfaces through OSINT, analysing network vulnerabilities, and creating malicious code required significant time — AI can process and analyse these instantly.

AI deepfakes aid social engineering attacks. By generating false images, videos, and voice phishing, AI makes phishing attempts more convincing by adapting content with contextual plausibility. This increases the likelihood of victims providing unauthorised access to threat actors.

AI introduces a new attack surface due to the possibility of its systems being exploited. Threat actors can instruct AI systems through prompt injection to behave contrary to their intended purpose or act maliciously.

How AI supports cyber security?

AI improves vulnerability management by guiding security decisions through detailed information and risk assessment, helping organisations prioritise risk levels effectively.

AI transforms security operation centres by efficiently investigating suspicious activities, analysing security events to detect anomalies, correlating events, and identifying patterns. By analysing behavioural patterns across large datasets, it streamlines UEBA (User and Entity Behaviour Analytics).

AI supports incident response by enabling security researchers to investigate time logs of suspicious activity. It also accelerates threat hunting by providing valuable insights into subtle patterns that may otherwise remain undetected.

AI Security Safeguards

  • Ensure private data or projects are never shared or discussed with any AI entity.
  • Monitor AI systems regularly for vulnerabilities.
  • AI may hallucinate — ensure it does not replace cyber security judgement.
